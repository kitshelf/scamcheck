# The recovery branch

For the user who already replied, paid, or sent documents. The screen runs first — the family, the markers, the label — because the finding still matters: it confirms what happened and feeds the report channels. Then this branch extends the report.

**The register is a nurse, not a judge.** No blame anywhere. The pattern is industrial; the person it caught is not the fool. Steps and channels only: no odds, no legal or financial advice, nothing filed on anyone's behalf.

## The moves, in order of urgency

1. **Money sent — call the sending institution now.** The window is short and shrinks hourly.
   - **Bank transfer / wire:** call the bank and ask them to recall the transfer. `[CONFIRM: wire or app transfer?]`
   - **Card:** dispute the charge with the card issuer.
   - **Gift cards:** call the number printed on the card; some unspent balances are recoverable.
   - **Crypto:** report to the exchange used and to `ic3.gov` (the FBI's complaint process exists partly for this); be honest that recovery is uncommon.
   - **P2P app (Zelle/CashApp/Venmo):** contact the app's support and your bank; outcomes vary by app and timing.
2. **Report it — the user files, not the kit.** FTC (`reportfraud.ftc.gov`) and IC3 (`ic3.gov`). Minutes of work; the report number helps the bank's case.
3. **Credentials or a one-time code shared:** change the password on that account from a clean device, turn on two-factor, and check for changes to recovery email/phone.
4. **ID documents or an SSN sent:** identitytheft.gov for the recovery plan, and a credit freeze at the three US bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). `[CONFIRM: what documents went out]`
5. **A check deposited:** tell the bank *before* it bounces — the user owes the bank the money it fronted. Do not send any more money to the other party.

## What the report says

- The trigger, stated plainly ("a payment was already sent").
- The moves above as a checklist, `[CONFIRM]` where the channel is unknown.
- The report channels, as pointers.
- That recovery is time-sensitive and that the kit cannot promise an outcome.

## What the report never says

- Never "you should have checked first," or any version of it.
- Never a probability of recovery beyond the honest "the window is short."
- Never a legal or financial recommendation — no "sue," no "you are entitled to."
- Never a filed complaint, a sent message, or a made call. The user acts; the kit points.
